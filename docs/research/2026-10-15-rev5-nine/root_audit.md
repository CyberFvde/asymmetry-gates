## GFS: distinguish the government investment from a personal trade

The verified event concerns a **U.S. Department of Commerce investment**, not evidence of a personal Donald Trump purchase. The [July 29 issuer announcement](https://gf.com/news-and-events/news/globalfoundries-signs-letter-of-intent-with-the-us-department-of-commerce-for-a-300-million-award-to-accelerate-us-silicon-photonics-leadership/) describes the silicon-photonics award and separate government equity participation. The September 3 [securities issuance agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1709048/000170904826000239/exhibit44-fx3asr.htm) specifies **9,907,399 initial shares**; the [registration statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1709048/000170904826000239/globalfoundriesincf-3asr.htm) identifies Commerce as the selling shareholder. This is financing issuance, not a tiny stock-funded acquisition under the new core carve-out.

The [Oct 14 NIST announcement](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/department-commerce-announces-agreement-globalfoundries-amend-its-existing) amends/reallocates up to $955M within the existing award rather than increasing its total. Separately, the [Oct 14 issuer announcement](https://gf.com/news-and-events/news/globalfoundries-selected-to-operate-two-new-semiconductor-fabs-in-new-york-under-landmark-us-japan-initiative/) describes GlobalFoundries operating proposed U.S.–Japan fabs, with a first fab expected in 2029. Neither headline establishes present organic cash growth or waives the capex/debt tests. No claim about a personal trade is inferred without its own disclosure. Do not adopt the draft's additional 4% share figure without a traced issuance source.
