## How the two screens were reconciled

Do not average the two sets of seed numbers. First establish delivered revenue or an explicit clinical exception, a material event inside the contest window, funding and dilution risks, and what success the current price already assumes. Prefer operating proof when opportunities are otherwise close. A strong company can remain expensive, and a skill pass alone does not establish a better March return. A technical achievement does not establish the probability of a favorable stock-price response.

PODD rises from third to first for the operating recovery/value case, with November 4 confirmed by the issuer. AVAV rises from sixth to second on funded demand and a second-half recovery that can be tested before March. LLY rises from eleventh to third on actual metabolic-drug earnings and its dated pipeline/meeting sequence. GPCR falls from first to fourth and from 25% to 10% of paper capital: it retains specific Q4 readouts, while Phase 3 topline remains in 2028. HWM and GNRC are added. RKLB drops from second to ninth because a Q4 pad target is not a guaranteed maiden flight and its March base requires an expensive sales multiple merely to hold the current price.

## Options decision

Shares are the default instrument for this combined paper basket. The prior RKLB/PL/QBTS calls lose approximately 91%/89%/82% of premiums in their modeled base cases. Merely shrinking the prior basket from 45% to 5% premiums changes its states to −48.5% / +7.3% / +101.8%, versus −46.3% / +12.0% / +92.1% at zero premiums. That is a cost/upside tradeoff, not demonstrated superiority.

The historical LLY April call looks better in one base sensitivity, but only seven contracts of open interest, a wide displayed spread and omitted dividend/American-model details prevent adopting it here. PODD’s March 19 expiry precedes the assumed contest end; its later cached chain has wide spreads. Current executable multi-leg quotes and contract specifications are missing for HWM/AVAV. No precise call or debit-spread recommendation is invented to fill a leverage allocation. Before adding an instrument, compare its March mark in every stock state, spreads, dilution/dividend effects, catalyst delay and contest scoring—not only its expiry payoff.

The exact prize rules could change the preferred tail exposure. Maximizing expected ending value, probability of first place and risk-adjusted return are different objectives. No name-specific outcome probabilities or competitors’ return distributions are established here. The comparison corrects the old sizing claim without inventing calibrated probabilities.
