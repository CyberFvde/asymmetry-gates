# FT — Stricter fintech/payment overlay (Oct 2, 2026, revision 2)

The user prefers tougher standards and lower priority for fintech/payments. Apply this overlay to payments networks/processors, fintech, crypto platforms, lenders and brokers, and to groups with material embedded finance. Enterprise software is not fintech merely because banks are its customers. For hybrid companies, disclose the finance exposure and apply the accounting/risk checks to the whole group; do not hide financial risk under e-commerce or software labels.

- **FT1 — No lenient routes.** Require full A–C or the existing fully passed #10 exception. Standard #9 uses the original ≥15% forward 3Y revenue CAGR AND ≥15% next-FY organic growth, rather than the general 12% next-FY threshold. No funded #14a starters and no G/H entry. A narrow shortfall may be an unfunded alert. A strong fintech can still qualify; do not manufacture a failure from the sector preference.
- **FT2 — Cash quality first.** Reconcile reported OCF to company cash generation excluding customer/deposit/segregated-fund movements, lending originations/repayments and funding flows as appropriate. Reconcile revenue quality and credit/fraud losses using the issuer's disclosures. Do not pass A3 on an OCF windfall from client money or a loan-book movement. If an auditable company-FCF basis cannot be established, status is unresolved and zero new capital. Show reported and normalized figures, with reasons and no arbitrary addbacks. This is the active fintech accounting requirement; pending P9 in a separate roster branch is not treated as adopted or fully specified.
- **FT3 — Balance-sheet and regulatory resilience.** A4 still governs non-bank operating businesses on the normalized basis. For a regulated bank/broker or a material credit book, also verify issuer-disclosed regulatory capital/liquidity compliance and a quantified buffer under a 25% increase in reported credit/fraud losses or the nearest disclosed relevant stress. Do not apply industrial debt/FCF blindly to customer-funded balance sheets or substitute low net debt for solvency. If the normalized A3/A4 interpretation or necessary sector stress cannot be supported, keep the name unresolved with no capital; generic industrial ratios alone cannot establish eligibility. Unknown material legal exposure, dependence on a crypto/trading boom, or management-adjusted cash claims cannot be waved through because growth is high.
- **FT4 — Evidence before priority.** Require two completed FYs of positive normalized company FCF and a base-case business case that does not need higher crypto prices, trading volume, rate spreads or looser underwriting merely to clear the gates. Compare per-share cash growth, dilution, take rates, customer retention, fraud/credit trends and regulatory concentration. Apply #7/#15/#19 in full. Document a measurable risk trigger for the dominant credit, liquidity, volume or regulatory dependency.
- **FT5 — Ranking.** Screen requested fintech names honestly and show passing evidence as well as weaknesses. When otherwise comparable opportunities qualify, rank non-fintech first in accordance with the user's preference. Sector dislike is a priority preference, not evidence that a company is bad or must fail.

The mandatory sell-to-cover interpretation applies here too: a verified non-discretionary tax transaction is not an insider-conviction signal, while a code-S market sale remains part of annual net selling. A corrected trade classification never excuses another failing cash, valuation or legal test. PH also applies: a recovered, fully FT-qualified name may use PH's initial staging limit, but this does not permit a funded #14a near-pass or waive FT qualification.
